## Forming an Endless team

A good Endless team needs a lot of support. As you climb, [tokens](#tokens) stack higher and higher, and picking a carry is hard if you don't know how to build the rest of the team. Two things matter most:

- **Passives are extremely important**, and you can **only** get them on starters. Bring as many useful passives as you can (the sections below say which).
- **Useful egg moves.** Dark Void matters most: in the wild only one legendary has it (the same goes for Core Enforcer and Fiery Wrath). If you want those moves, *bring them*.

The starters recommended in this guide are good mainly for those two reasons. Everything else, even moves like Mat Block, can be found during the run.

You can throw together an average team on a whim. An effective one has **a mapped-out plan** for the early, mid and late stages. A team core is one carry, one waiter, one runner, one boss killer and a support. The remaining slots bring specific moves or parts.

What to start with (credit to Grex):

- **[Runner](#runner):** a Pokémon with the Run Away **passive**.
- **[Waiter](#waiter):** a Pokémon with a lure **passive** (No Guard, Illuminate, Commander or Arena Trap).
- **[Boss killer](#boss-killer):** a Pokémon with the Purifying Salt **or** Sturdy **passive**.
- **[Carry](#carry):** one that can hold its own and kill in doubles with a single attack that has no immunities.
- **[Fuse tutors](#tutor):** Pokémon that learn or have Dark Void or Mat Block.
- **[Support](#sturdy-support):** a Pokémon with the Psychic Surge **passive**.

## Carry

You only need **one**.

The carry is a strong early-game Pokémon that easily beats opponents through the first thousand waves. It has to survive comfortably until your late-game team is complete.

A good carry has a strong attack with no type immunities, an ability that boosts its damage, and enough speed to strike first. The first names that come to mind are Necrozma, Zacian or Eternatus, but they often do badly in Endless: they favour single battles, have bad typing, or awful movesets.

Two things separate elite carries from average ones. First, a very strong early game: self-sufficient by the first Paradox without form-change items or late evolutions. Second, and more important, **usefulness after the early game**: the carry can turn into a tool for shiny hunting, boss killing, weather control and so on.

### The ideal carry moveset

- A spread move with no immunities, for double battles.
- Endure, to set up, mainly on Paradox and Eternatus waves.
- A single-target move with no immunities, to kill a shiny's partner in a double battle.
- Your choice, often a move for late game, like Gastro Acid on a Mold Breaker Pokémon.

## Waiter

![A finished waiter: Gourgeist with Powder Snow, Dark Void, Endure and Mat Block](shots/waiter.png)

Waiters protect your other Pokémon and help you catch the shinies and legendaries you want.

The usual waiters are Mienshao, Watchog, Garchomp and Gourgeist; weaker options are Gigantamax Flapple and Dracozolt. They are all fast and all have **lure passives**, which the triple lure setup **requires**.

**What are lure abilities?** Abilities that act like the Lure item while the Pokémon is on the field: No Guard, Commander, Illuminate and Arena Trap. **Why use them?** More lures means more Pokémon, which means more shinies.

**Setup.** A waiter runs Dark Void, Mat Block and Powder Snow, with the fourth move up to you (Endure, Quick Guard, Wide Guard, Disable, Torment, Fake Out, Gastro Acid). For these to work it needs a lot of speed: a +Speed nature, 10 Soul Dews and 31 Carbos.

**Why these moves?**

- **Mat Block** protects the user and its ally from all damaging moves, if it's fast enough. It only works on a Pokémon's first turn in battle, like Fake Out. It's one guaranteed safe turn, unless you face Urshifu or get hit by Feint, Hyperspace Hole or Mighty Cleave.
- **Dark Void** is an 80% accurate move that puts both targets to sleep; the waiter needs No Guard to make it always hit. They sleep, eat a Lum Berry at the end of the turn, and you do it again: three turns of stall before they can wake up at random.
- **Powder Snow** is a spread move with no immunities and a 10% freeze chance per hit. With 3 King's Rocks it flinches as often as Rock Slide, plus the freeze chance.

Once the waiter has 31 Carbos and its moves, fuse it with a Pokémon that has a **different** lure ability. One of the two must be No Guard. The faster the Pokémon you fuse with, the better.

## Runner

![A finished runner: Teddiursa fused with Illuminate, with Thief, Gastro Acid, Whirlwind and Torment](shots/runner.png)

**Why Run Away?** Brute force is painfully slow: [Protection tokens](#tokens) eventually stack so high that an ordinary battle can take ten minutes and only reward you with the shop. Running is the fastest phase of shiny hunting in Endless, and where most epic Pokémon are found and caught.

Once your team is set up, you switch to Pokémon with Run Away, ideally as a **passive**. It guarantees an escape from any wild battle, except Paradox waves and Neutralizing Gas.

**Why a passive?** So you can find a **wild** Pokémon with a lure ability and splice it onto your runner. That's essential for triple lure, the best setup for shiny hunting.

The best runner right now is **Teddiursa**: Honey Gather ability, Run Away passive, and a big support movepool from TMs. Late in the run its ability is replaced with a lure.

**Setup.** The core moves are Gastro Acid, Whirlwind or Roar, and Torment, with the last slot your choice: Core Enforcer, Ruination, Protect, Fake Out, Soak, Thief. Thief is used in the carry phase for extra eggs, and can be swapped out before you start running, since catching Pokémon gets you their eggs. Torment or Disable handles multi-hit moves on Paradox Pokémon.

## Sturdy support

This Pokémon makes DoT kills much faster, lets you kill Grass types, and lets you pivot safely back into your waiter after the fight, forcing a double battle right away. It also brings either Mold Breaker Gastro Acid or Psychic Surge, depending on the passive you build it with.

### With Psychic Surge

![Solosis with Protect, Explosion, Soak and Heal Block](shots/support-psychic-surge.png)

The best Psychic Surge **passive** is **Solosis**. It gets Protect and Heal Block by level-up and Explosion by TM, costs 3 points (1 with two cost reductions), needs no egg moves and has shiny variants. Its moves: Heal Block, Soak, Protect and a [pivot move](#words-we-use).

- **Heal Block** stops the enemy side healing from any source for 5 turns, including berries and Recovery tokens.
- **Soak** makes the target Water type. It removes a Grass type so Leech Seed works, and doubles Salt Cure from 1/16 to 1/8 of the target's HP.
- **Protect** for when you don't heal to full, or to dodge a multi-hit attack.
- **A pivot move** gets you safely back to the waiter. On the turn the boss will die to your DoT, use it: the boss attacks, you faint or switch, the waiter comes in, the boss dies, and the waiter is on the field to force a double battle right away.

**Why Psychic Surge?** It turns off other terrains (Electric and Misty stop grounded Pokémon falling asleep; Grassy heals grounded Pokémon 1/16 of their HP), and it blocks single-target priority moves against grounded Pokémon, including priority from abilities.

### With Mold Breaker

![Primal Kyogre with Fishious Rend, Soak, Gastro Acid and Heal Block](shots/support-mold-breaker.png)

With a Mold Breaker passive it keeps Heal Block, Soak and a pivot move, but the fourth move is **Gastro Acid**, which suppresses the target's ability and passive.

**Why Mold Breaker?** For two abilities:

- **Good as Gold** makes every status move aimed at the user fail. Gholdengo is the only Pokémon met in the wild with it.
- **Magic Bounce** bounces most status moves back. The most notable user is Hatterene, because it's a Fairy type; Xatu, Espeon and Uxie get it too. Skill Swap and Curse aren't bounced.

Hatterene being Fairy means Core Enforcer can't remove its ability, unlike the others above.

**Which should I use?** Whichever you have the parts for; you can find or build the other mid-run. For Psychic Surge, fuse Psychic Terrain onto a Prankster Pokémon, or find a wild Indeedee (hidden ability) or Tapu Lele. For Mold Breaker, fuse Gastro Acid onto a Mold Breaker Pokémon.

## Prankster support

The Prankster support is used in lots of situations, with a flexible moveset. Usually you build it from wild Pokémon since it needs no special ability, but you can start with one for luck or a specific Pokémon.

Prankster gives status moves +1 priority. It fails against Dark types, against grounded foes in Psychic Terrain, and against abilities that block priority like Dazzling. That limits it, but plenty still works:

- No Psychic Surge Pokémon, or tired of status conditions? Give it **Psychic Terrain** or **Misty Terrain**, or both.
- Want a faster waiter without more eggs? **Tailwind** doubles its speed.
- Tired of rerolling for Reviver Seeds? **Disable** or **Torment** stop multi-hit moves, and **Gastro Acid** stops Mold Breaker (these target foes, so they can fail as above).
- Does your carry need help early? **After You**, **Decorate**, **Life Dew**, **Lunar Blessing**, **Gravity**.
- **Revival Blessing** or **Speed Swap** work too.

For Skill Swap or Entrainment with Serene Grace you **need a starter with the Prankster passive**: see the [Serene Grace build](#post-berry-rework-serene-grace).

## Boss killer

As tokens scale you can no longer one-shot Paradoxes the normal way. You **must** switch to a late-game strategy: run from most battles, and use a **boss killer** on the battles you can't skip. Boss killers use indirect or percentage damage, which *ignores tokens*, so they win at *any wave*.

You usually switch from your carry to a boss killer between waves 1000 and 2000, earlier or later depending on you and how fast you assemble the team, and keep using it for the rest of the run. This guide focuses on DoT, but covers others too.

## Damage over time

**What is DoT and why use it?** DoT uses moves like Curse and Salt Cure that deal set damage, which bypasses Protection tokens. It's the easiest Endless build to learn, and very effective. Easy doesn't mean bad.

**How to build it.** You need two Pokémon, a **boss killer** and a **support**, both with Sturdy as ability or passive. The boss killer sets up every DoT, so it should have an ability or passive that helps it get them off. Purifying Salt stops status, mainly paralysis, so it can't be fully paralysed before Leech Seed. On an Electric boss killer Inner Focus is ideal: it can't be paralysed or flinched, so nothing stops Leech Seed.

Good starters for DoT (but **any** Sturdy or Purifying Salt **passive** works):

- ![Yamask](sprite:yamask) **Yamask.** Purifying Salt passive, low 38 HP, Ghost type, Curse and Protect by level-up, no egg moves needed, shiny variants, 3 points. Once its moves are done, fuse it with any wild Sturdy Pokémon.
- ![Pyukumuku](sprite:pyukumuku) **Pyukumuku.** Purifying Salt passive, low 55 HP, Curse by level-up, Salt Cure and Baneful Bunker as egg moves, shiny variants, 2 points. Not Ghost, so it needs a Tera Ghost shard. It only needs Leech Seed, then a fusion with any wild Sturdy Pokémon.
- ![Pichu](sprite:pichu-spiky-eared) **Spiky-eared Pichu** (not normal Pichu). Sturdy passive, 20 HP, Electric so it can't be paralysed, Protect and Curse as common TMs. It doesn't need Purifying Salt, so give it Inner Focus to stop flinches. The best fusion is **Annihilape**, Ghost secondary with Inner Focus. Keep Lum Berries on it so poison and burn don't kill it.

![A finished boss killer: Pyukumuku fusion with Curse, Spiky Shield, Salt Cure and Leech Seed](shots/boss-killer-pyukumuku.png)

Its moves: **Curse, Salt Cure, Leech Seed, and a Protect-like move.**

**Items.** Only 2 Wide Lens, so Leech Seed always hits. Leech Seed plus Protect heals you to full. If you're flinched on turn 1, reload and Protect on turn 1 to move the game's RNG forward. To be fully safe, add 4 Leftovers and Sitrus Berries: with 5 Healing Charms that heals you to full (37.5% from the Sitrus, 37.5% from Leftovers, then Protect for Leftovers to top you up). The Pichu/Annihilape fusion can't be flinched, so it always gets Leech Seed off, but it needs Lum Berries against poison and burn. If your boss killer isn't Ghost type, **Terastallize Ghost a turn before using Curse**, or you'll curse your own Pokémon.

### How it kills bosses

With a standard build, your waiter starts the battle. Switch into a Pokémon that won't help this fight and sac it, to bring the boss killer in safely.

1. **Leech Seed first**, because healing to full matters for Sturdy.
2. If you didn't heal to full, **Protect**. If you did, **Salt Cure**, then **Curse** (Tera Ghost the turn before if needed). Curse makes the boss killer faint.
3. Bring in the **support** and use **Heal Block**, **Soak** and **Protect** until the turn the boss will die.
4. On that turn use the **pivot move**. The boss attacks, your Pokémon faints or switches, the waiter comes in safely, then the boss dies.

Your waiter, ideally with two lure abilities, is on the field when the battle ends, so a double battle starts right away.

Against a **Grass** type it's slightly different: after the sac, bring the support in to use Soak first, sac another Pokémon, bring the boss killer in, and carry on as normal.

## Metal Burst

**Girafarig** and **Wynaut** are the best starters for Metal Burst. For the final build, these are the two best Metal Burst fusions:

![Wobbuffet fused with Mega Kangaskhan: Counter, Mirror Coat, Protect, Metal Burst](shots/metal-burst-wobbuffet.png)

**Wobbuffet**

- Has Comeuppance (like Metal Burst, slightly worse because it makes contact).
- Must be fused with **Mega Kangaskhan** for Parental Bond. Kangaskhan is hard to find in the wild, so you may want to start with one.

![Farigiraf with Sturdy: Protect, Counter, Metal Burst, Dragon Tail](shots/metal-burst-farigiraf.png)

**Farigiraf**

- Less HP, so less Metal Burst damage, but other advantages.
- Easier to build: fuse it with *any wild Sturdy Pokémon*. Aggron gives Metal Burst, Counter and Sturdy at once; or learn the moves first and then use a high-HP Sturdy Pokémon.
- Can Tera Electric to avoid paralysis (it isn't a Mega). Otherwise Paralysis tokens stack to a 25% chance to paralyse you.

**Moves**

- **Metal Burst / Comeuppance.** Your damage. Use Multi Lens and Parental Bond.
- **Protect.** Get back to full HP for Sturdy, or stall a turn.
- **Counter.** For Iron Hands, whose Focus Punch makes Metal Burst go first and do nothing.
- **An extra move.** Anything, but these are good: phazing moves to isolate shinies, Gastro Acid or Core Enforcer to remove abilities, Mirror Coat for extra damage on Eternatus.

**Parental Bond is highly recommended**: it doubles the damage of Counter and Metal Burst. With two Multi Lens the hits are 50/25/25/100.

Metal Burst and Comeuppance don't have negative priority like other counter moves, so use a **−Speed nature with 9+ Soul Dews**.

You **must** run **Counter** because of *Iron Hands*. Its Focus Punch (priority −3) always goes after Metal Burst. Counter has priority −5, so it goes after Focus Punch and kills Iron Hands.

People often Tera Electric so the Paralysis token can't paralyse them (up to 25% chance). You can't Terastallize a Mega.

**Never put Quick Claws on your Metal Burster.**

### The Metal Burst support

Metal Burst doesn't need extra help on ??? fights, so your support looks different: no Soak for Leech Seed, and it can use Nature's Madness or Ruination for more damage. But Metal Burst struggles to catch X0 shinies alone (you can't choose who attacks you, and can't use Roar), so instead you build a Pokémon that lets you catch X0 shinies. Its moves:

- **Nature's Madness / Ruination.** Halves the target's current HP. Boss shields reduce it, but Multi Lens (and Parental Bond) make up for it: with 2 Multi Lens it's on par with Curse.
- **Heal Block.** Stops Recovery tokens, berries, Matcha Gotcha and other draining or healing for 5 turns, by which time the target has usually fainted.
- **Curse.** Used by a Ghost type, it costs half your max HP and puts a permanent DoT of 1/4 of the target's max HP on it each turn.
- **Gastro Acid.** With Mold Breaker, suppresses Good as Gold, Magic Bounce and other problem abilities and passives.

The first three together kill a boss in 4 to 5 turns, depending on its shields. This Pokémon doesn't need Sturdy, since it's only used on X0 waves with the waiter protecting it, but a Tera Ghost shard gives it Ghost type if needed. Ideally it has Psychic Surge and Mold Breaker (or similar), which makes it useful outside X0 waves too.
