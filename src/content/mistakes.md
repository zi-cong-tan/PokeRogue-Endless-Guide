## What not to do

Why not use Well-Baked Body, or Zacian? Which Tera type should you use? Can you just bring a pile of shinies and a carry? This chapter covers what to avoid.

## Well-Baked Body

![Zacian with Well-Baked Body, Play Rough, Iron Head, Bitter Blade and Lunge](shots/well-baked-body.png)

A Fairy/Steel Pokémon with **Well-Baked Body** (usually from Dachsbun) or **Flash Fire** (usually from Heatran) seems good because it walls Eternatus. It's a massive **noob trap**.

You face Eternatus only 23 times in Endless: **99.6% of the run** is non-Eternatus waves. Beyond the very early game, which should be easy anyway, your Well-Baked Body Pokémon is useless on all of them.

Worse, Eternatus isn't even a hard boss. Its stats barely matter once tokens stack, and it has nothing special going on. SturdyBurst, DoT, even Shade Toss beat it, and they also beat every Paradox and every other Pokémon. **Well-Baked Body strategies only beat what other strategies already beat**, and nothing new, so they waste a team slot and DNA Splicers.

If early Eternatus is giving you trouble, use early versions of your later strategy (lead with a Sturdy + Salt Cure Pokémon, chip it with Wobbuffet) or low-commitment tricks (bait Eternabeam by sacking Pokémon, use Spore from a Ledian you were already bringing), instead of a whole slot and several Pokémon that only help against early Eternatus.

Still not convinced? Fairy/Steel Well-Baked Body won't even beat Eternatus forever. Around wave 2000, 3000 at most, you won't out-damage the [Recovery tokens](#tokens) any more. You'll spend dozens of turns PP-stalling it and it will 6-0 you with Struggle. Whatever you do, you need a real endgame strategy for late Eternatus, so build that from the start.

![Protection token: reduces incoming damage by 2.5%](shots/token-protection.png)

![Damage token: increases damage by 5%](shots/token-damage.png)

These tokens multiply and go up to 999, until you deal **1** damage and are always one-shot.

## Candy Jars

On paper Candy Jars sound fine: each jar makes Rare Candies give one more level, so with 99 jars a Rare Candy gives 100 levels.

They're **very bad**. Each jar costs a reward pick you could spend on Master Balls, DNA Splicers or money. Maxing them out takes 99 reward picks, and you can't pick a reward every 10th wave. Then you have to keep finding Rare Candies to make the investment pay off.

You waste a lot of time, money and rewards on jars and candies you don't need. Sitting 5000 levels above the cap is funny, but you never need to be above the cap to clear Endless, and massive overleveling won't get you to 5850 without an endgame strategy. Brute force alone never wins Endless. The record is wave 5000 ([Discord message with the video](https://discord.com/channels/1125469663833370665/1461342528568688694/1466825234911854674)).

**Eggs are much better.** Each Lucky Egg gives 40% more Exp and each Golden Egg 100%. 99 Lucky Eggs (easy from catching wild Pokémon holding them, Covet on bosses, Magician, Pickup) give 3960% more Exp per battle, and 12 Golden Eggs (the minimum recommended) another 1200%: just short of 8000% more per battle, and 99 Golden Eggs alone give 9900%. That's a lot of levels **without** jars or Rare Candies. You don't need to be at or above the cap; you only need enough speed on the waiter, or enough HP on a SturdyBurster.

## Wasting starting slots

Your starting team is extremely important: starters are the only source of passives, and they're how you bring rare moves like Dark Void and Mat Block.

A good team gets you many shinies. **A team of random shinies is not a good team.** A typical semi-random team: a carry; a second carry in case the first dies; something with Pickup ("good, right?"); something with no use besides luck; and something because you heard the word "Trenacl" somewhere.

You can beat Endless with that. You can beat it with only wild Pokémon. But passives make Endless easier and shiny hunting better, and starting moves matter: [Dark Void](#tutor) is so useful that players bring it from the start, because in the wild only Darkrai has it, a super rare boss in the Abyss.

Wasting starting slots is the best way to make Endless harder for yourself. You're better off with little or no luck and a good team than SSS luck and a bad team. Luck comes mid-run; sometimes you even catch a shiny with Sturdy for your boss killer. Useful passives (Run Away, Purifying Salt / Sturdy, the [lures](#waiter)) make the run easier and shiny hunting better.

## Pokérus

**What does it do?** Pokérus gives 1.5x Exp and has a 1 in 10 chance to spread to another party Pokémon each wave. You only get it by picking one of the 5 random starters that have it, and they change daily.

**Why is it bad?** Pokérus itself isn't bad: it helps early and means fewer eggs later. What's bad is picking a **useless** Pokémon just for it. A useful Pokémon beats a useless one with Pokérus. If a good Pokémon for your build has it that day, great, but don't go out of your way.

**Is it needed?** No. Lucky and Golden Eggs give enough Exp. It saves a few eggs but won't change how you play.

## The wrong Tera

**What does Tera Stellar do?** It boosts all your STAB moves by 50% and non-STAB moves by 20%, but only once each. A normal Tera type, like Water, boosts every Water move by 50% for as long as you're that type.

**Why do Tera types matter?** Tera is a 50% boost to that type, so match it to the spread move you spam. Tera Stellar is bad on every Pokémon, because you only ever spam one move. The exception is Terapagos: it gains 100 base stats and Tera Starstorm becomes a Stellar spread move, but it's still not great, since you rely on finding a Tera Orb and it doesn't do much for **9** points. Use the Tera that boosts the spread move you're **always** clicking, even when it's 4x resisted.

For DoT you **always** run Tera Ghost, unless your DoT Pokémon is already part Ghost. For Metal Burst you'll likely use Tera Electric to avoid losing turns to the Paralysis token.

## Physical attackers

**Why is Zacian disliked by good Endless players?** It isn't. We just don't recommend it, because it's a bad carry. Koraidon, Rayquaza, Necrozma and others share its problems, but newer players use Zacian most. You can use a weaker carry; it just takes more skill and effort, and newer players don't have much Endless experience yet.

Why most physical attackers are bad:

- **Intimidate.** Most can't stop it, and plenty of Pokémon have it. Zacian gets +1 Attack from its ability, but that won't save it from the dozen Gyarados in the Lake, and dropping from +1 to +0 still costs a lot of damage.
- **Contact.** Many abilities trigger on contact. Flame Body burns (halving Attack), Static paralyses (halving speed and losing turns), Gooey lowers speed. Iron Barbs and Rough Skin chip you. Ferrothorn has both: a contact move costs you a quarter of your HP, and if an Endure token procs you hit again and lose another quarter. Ferrothorn isn't rare, so a few in a row, or two in a double battle against a contact spread move like Brutal Swing, can kill you. Not every physical move makes contact, so this matters less than Intimidate, but it comes up.
- **Spread moves.** There are fewer physical spread moves, and fewer still with no immunities. Glacial Lance and Diamond Storm don't make contact and have no immunities, but each is learned by one Pokémon in the wild. Thousand Arrows is the only Ground move that hits Flying, but Earth Eater blocks it, and it's also one Pokémon. Rock Slide is common, spread and has no immunities, but hits weaker; that's why people say Tera Rock Rock Slide.
- **Raw power.** The special spread moves we use hit harder. Water Spout and Eruption have 150 power, Glacial Lance 120, Diamond Storm 100. Even common ones: Dazzling Gleam 80, Rock Slide 75. Fiery Wrath has 90, and good special attackers like Darkrai, Greninja, Yveltal and Hoopa get it, while the only good physical carries with Thousand Arrows (90 power) are Zygarde and Excadrill, and it can't hit Earth Eater without Mold Breaker.

**Which physical attackers are good?** The ones that get around those problems:

- ![Excadrill](sprite:excadrill) **Excadrill** isn't a bad carry: Mold Breaker hidden ability, Sturdy passive, Thousand Arrows as a rare egg move. Mold Breaker means no immunities, and Mold Breaker + Sturdy means it becomes your [support](#sturdy-support) after the carry phase.
- ![Regigigas](sprite:regigigas) **Regigigas** is the best physical attacker. Normalize turns its egg move Thousand Waves (a spread move without contact) into a Normal move, giving STAB and a 1.2x boost. Its Scrappy passive hits Ghosts, so no immunities, and blocks Intimidate. It's also a core part of Grex's [Entrainment](#normalise-entrainment) strategy.
- ![Kyurem](sprite:kyurem-black) **Kyurem-Black** has 170 Attack and Glacial Lance as an egg move, so Tera Ice Glacial Lance hits very hard. Mold Breaker ignores Sturdy early on. Its passive is useless, but base Kyurem's Snow Warning makes Blizzard 100% accurate. To make it a support later, bring a separate Sturdy passive, since its ability ignores others.

## Not being open-minded

The biggest problem players have is going into Endless thinking it's Classic without an end, with no plan, expecting to brute-force it. Endless plays very differently, and you need to know what you're doing to do well.

**Knowing how Endless works is the most important part.** Good players can win without the meta carries and Pokémon. If you understand how it works, you can make almost anything work.

![Wave 5850 victory: a run by Grex with no fusions](shots/victory-5850-a.png)

Grex did this run without any fusion, and still did well and caught a lot of shinies, because he knows what he's doing.

![Wave 5850 victory: Monkee's run using only Ground type Pokémon](shots/victory-5850-b.png)

Monkee has beaten Endless using only Ground types (and the same with Water, Normal and Bug), not just on the team but for fuse tutoring too, and beat it by brute force, which nobody had done before. (G-Max Copperajah is Ground type in this game.)

![A good starting team](shots/good-starting-team.png)

This is a good starting team. But if you can't tell *why* it's good, it's a terrible one. You can't just pick what people call good and expect an auto-win: you need to know each Pokémon's role, why it's there and why it's good.
