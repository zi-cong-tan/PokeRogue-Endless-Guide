## Standard triple lure DoT

The standard build: what it needs and how it's used.

**What you need**

- A [carry](#carry): a Pokémon that kills in doubles on its own with a spread move that has no immunities.
- A Pokémon with a lure passive: your [waiter](#waiter).
- A Pokémon with the Run Away passive: your [runner](#runner).
- A Pokémon with the Sturdy or Purifying Salt passive: your [boss killer](#boss-killer).
- A Pokémon with the Psychic Surge or Mold Breaker passive: your [support](#sturdy-support). Your carry can fill this role.
- A Pokémon with Dark Void or Mat Block: your [tutor](#tutor). Your carry can fill this role too; if it does, or it's also your support, you can bring two tutors.

**Example team**

- ![Kyogre](sprite:kyogre) Kyogre: carry, later the support.
- ![Pyukumuku](sprite:pyukumuku) Pyukumuku: boss killer.
- ![Teddiursa](sprite:teddiursa) Teddiursa: runner.
- ![Smeargle](sprite:smeargle) Smeargle: Dark Void tutor.
- ![Froakie](sprite:froakie) Froakie: Mat Block tutor.
- ![Pumpkaboo](sprite:pumpkaboo-average) Pumpkaboo: waiter.

**Setup.** Until you get [splicers](#fusing), just make your carry stronger: Carbos, Calcium or Protein, Soul Dews and other useful items.

With your first splicer, fuse **waiter + tutor**, in that order, learn the move (Dark Void or Mat Block), and unfuse. Use the free slot to look for moves to tutor onto your other Pokémon, like Leech Seed onto the boss killer.

If your carry will become your support, then after both tutors are fused away, catch a Pokémon with Sturdy and teach it the support's moves, so you can fuse it with the carry once everything else is set up. If your carry is a tutor, see [when to fuse it](#using-greninja-or-darkrai-as-your-carry).

![A finished standard build at wave 976: waiter, runner, boss killer and support set up](shots/finished-build-976.png)

Once the waiter, runner, support and boss killer look like this, you're ready to run. You can keep using your carry until the next Paradox or Eternatus and practise the setup there until you're comfortable, then start running.

## Floedinja (Dark Void)

**Valid until the berry rework (1.13).** Built by Pepo. *Basically: Floette + Shedinja.*

**Wonder Guard** is one of the only abilities that lets you live a hit that would otherwise defeat you. Unlike Sturdy, it only protects you from attacks that aren't super effective. No dual typing has zero weaknesses, and **Shedinja fusions can only Tera Bug**, so normally you can't fix that, unless you use a move that changes your type, like **Conversion**.

You need **a way to use Conversion fast**, before you're defeated: **Speed Swap** or **Trick Room**, used by something with **Sturdy**. That Pokémon gets a free turn after (it takes the hit, sets up, then outspeeds), so give it **Curse** for extra damage.

The Wonder Guard user can be Shedinja with Prankster (Smeadinja) or with the **Magic Guard passive**: Shedinja on its own, or fused with an **Eternal Floette** that has the passive. Fairy/Ghost typing plus Tera Bug lets Floedinja counter almost every boss without even using Conversion, with rare exceptions like Flutter Mane with Mystical Fire and a Ghost move. Because of Fusion tokens you should still pack Conversion for bad matchups.

Its moves need to deal damage fast, since its only help is Curse from the support:

- **Heal Block** is a must.
- **Nature's Madness** hits hard and gives a good Fairy typing to Conversion into.
- A move whose type gives **full immunity to any four moves**: Poison, Ground, Water, Electric or Dragon. Whirlpool or Clamp add DoT, but Nature's Madness covers that, so Flip Turn, Magnet Rise or even Sand Attack (relearned from Shedinja with a Memory Mushroom) work. Burn Up or Double Shock make you typeless, but that's mostly unnecessary. Encore is also an option to abuse Tera.

**Floette** is no Reshiram. Light of Ruin hits very hard, especially with its Mega Stone, but it's single-target, so don't take lures until you find a **Dazzling Gleam** TM, and maybe wait longer if your damage isn't high enough yet.

**Nincada** learns the moves Floedinja needs without spending Floette's move slots, and duplicates items when it evolves: perfect when you're ready to run but missing a few Soul Dews, and good for Golden Eggs.

**Teddiursa** is the same as ever: **Gastro Acid** and **Roar / Whirlwind** are required. The other two can be Dragon Tail, Core Enforcer, Torment or Protect.

**Gourgeist** gets as many **Carbos** and **Soul Dews** as possible, enough **Golden Eggs** to keep up with levels, and **Mat Block, Powder Snow, Dark Void and Quick Guard / Protect**. Quick Guard is your only answer to priority unless you build another Pokémon mid-run.

**Polteageist** matters most in the boss-killing phase, but its Sturdy passive helps chip foes Floette struggles with early. Late game, **Ghost typing plus a Sturdy passive** is very convenient. Without it, a Mold Breaker support meant Tera Ghost Excadrill or a wild hidden-ability Basculegion.

**Pokérus** helps in the carry phase. The hardest part of the run is waves 50 to 250, where Exp is scarce. It also means your waiter needs fewer Golden Eggs. **Smeargle** can give Sketch to Nincada or Gourgeist, saving 2 to 3 splicers, and Dark Void if you have it. **Porygon** brings Dazzling Gleam (egg move) and Conversion, if you don't want to gamble on the TM.

### Game plan

**Early game: the carry phase.** Floette in the first slot. The rest doesn't matter, but **Teddiursa helps with Fake Out** in the occasional double battle. Avoid double battles until you have Dazzling Gleam, unless you started with **Watchog**, whose Instruct hits twice. Floette runs **Dazzling Gleam, Light of Ruin and Endure** for the carry phase.

If you brought a **tutor**, use your **first splicer on it** to free the slot. If you brought **Pokérus**, catch a tutor for any move **as soon as Pokérus spreads** to one other party member. Give Floette one or two Carbos and Gourgeist the rest.

Priorities, in no fixed order:

- **Mat Block and Dark Void on Gourgeist.**
- **Nincada's moves:** Conversion, Nature's Madness, and a [fourth move](#floedinjas-fourth-move) of your choice. Its last move, Heal Block, is learned by level-up as **Shedinja**.
- At least **Gastro Acid** and **Roar / Whirlwind** on Teddiursa.
- **TMs:** Dazzling Gleam and Endure for Floette, Trick Room for Polteageist, Roar for Teddiursa.

Then you're technically ready for the next phase. These make running safer if you do them first:

- As many **Carbos** and **Soul Dews** on Gourgeist as possible.
- **Gourgeist** and **Teddiursa** fused with lure abilities.
- **Polteageist** fused with a Mold Breaker Pokémon, and taught Gastro Acid by fusion.

When it's time to run, give **Nincada** all your items (except Fairy Feathers) and evolve it. **Fuse Floette and Shedinja.** That's your boss killer.

### Floedinja's fourth move

Floedinja wants a specific kind of move for full type immunity.

- **Double Shock / Burn Up** make it immune to almost any move, which is useful when Floedinja is catching a wild shiny.
- **Whirlpool** means Polteageist doesn't need a Reviver Seed to survive multi-hit moves: send in Floedinja and **solo the boss**, since most multi-hit users can't touch Floedinja with Tera.

You'll rarely meet an enemy that hits even two of your types, so this isn't required. Otherwise fill the slot with:

- **Encore.** The opponent can only repeat its last attack, usually a damaging one that can't hit you super effectively. It works like Taunt and Conversion at once, stopping it from clicking a threatening move.
- **Snap Trap / Infestation.** Whirlpool alternatives that Water Absorb can't block: a failsafe against fusions.
- Or experiment. Floedinja's moves are flexible (experienced players even drop Conversion for Encore), so you may find a better combination.

### Killing bosses

Against Paradoxes and Eternatus: switch to a [sac](#words-we-use), then bring in the **support** when it faints. It survives a hit with Sturdy, uses **Trick Room**, then **Curse**. If the boss has no Steel or Ghost moves, Curse right away.

Send in **Floedinja**. Use **Conversion** if the boss has a Steel or Ghost move; otherwise go straight to **Heal Block** to deny Sitrus and Enigma Berries and token healing. Then spam **Nature's Madness**; you'll need more of them as the boss segments go up. With Tera available, you can skip Conversion if the boss has no **Flying, Fire or Rock** move and go **Tera Bug into Heal Block**.

**If Curse will kill next turn**, switch to a sac so **Gourgeist** comes in safely, or switch straight to Gourgeist if the boss is spamming status moves (bosses pick a random move 1 time in 16, so only Flip Turn on Floedinja is fully safe, but that's niche). You want Gourgeist on the field so triple lure starts again immediately.

**Tip:** deep into the run you can **tell which attack types a boss has from its items**. A **Metal Coat** or **Poison Barb** means it can hit Floedinja. A **Sharp Beak**, **Hard Stone** or **Charcoal** means it can hit Tera Bug Floedinja too, so you'll need Conversion. **Eternatus can never hit Floedinja**, except Reshiram and Zekrom fusions with Turboblaze / Teravolt.

### Running

Like every other build: **spam Run with Gourgeist and Teddiursa on the field**, stopping to use **Mat Block + Roar to catch Pokémon you want**, such as shinies or the Pokémon you still need for the build.

On X0 waves Roar fails and you have to catch something, so stall with Gourgeist:

- Bring **Polteageist** in safely with Mat Block.
- Polteageist Curses the non-shiny; Gourgeist uses Dark Void / Powder Snow to protect both.
- Keep stalling with Gourgeist, bring **Floedinja** in and attack as usual. The enemy should fall in 3 attacks.

### What can go wrong

Beyond the usual [run enders](#neutralizing-gas), Floedinja has few once set up: mainly **Tachyon Cutter** from Iron Crown. A **Reviver Seed** on Teddiursa (for Torment / Disable) or Whirlpool on Floedinja covers it. **Neutralizing Gas / Mold Breaker fusions** that need Gastro Acid are another reason to keep a Reviver Seed on at all times.

A weakness of every Wonder Guard build: the opponent knowing **Entrainment** or **Gastro Acid**, which it uses once it realises it can't hit you. **Switching out and back in** fixes it, because it forgets you have Wonder Guard and you get a free turn or two. It's rare anyway, since it needs a fusion. **Encore** also shuts those moves down.

### Should you run it?

Floedinja is very safe, and fast for a DoT build:

- **No healing needed.** Polteageist doesn't need full HP, and everyone else faints in one hit if hit at all, skipping all the healing animations and text. Give Polteageist only Lum Berries, and give Floedinja **no** Lum Berries, or it will cure its own Burn / Poison every turn.
- **Faster boss kills** than the popular Leech Seed + Salt Cure + Curse + Heal Block + Soak cycle: Heal Block lands sooner and Nature's Madness is as strong as Curse or stronger. In the best (and most common) case, skipping Conversion and Trick Room, a boss falls in 4 turns.
- **Multi-hit moves** aren't a problem with a Reviver Seed on Polteageist or Whirlpool on Floedinja.
- It's very hard, maybe impossible, for your boss killer to be hit on X0 waves.

The downsides:

- Four Pokémon need passives, like any build. Gourgeist's and Teddiursa's are common, but Eternal Floette's is specific to this build and Polteageist isn't popular, so expect to grind those two.
- Eternal Floette isn't the easiest carry, especially without egg moves, so the carry phase can be hard.

**Teddiursa** needs **Gastro Acid** and **Roar / Whirlwind**, and here it benefits a lot from **Core Enforcer**, because there's no Mold Breaker slot (Core Enforcer shuts down Gholdengo; Magic Bounce Hatterene is less of a problem, since Curse + Nature's Madness handle it and Curse isn't reflected). ### Game plan

**Early game.** Floette first, the **Prankster** second (to protect with Mat Block / Friend Guard or support with Decorate). No double battles until you have Dazzling Gleam or Instruct. Floette runs Dazzling Gleam, Light of Ruin, Endure, and eventually Nature's Madness. Drop a tutor with your first splicer; with Pokérus, catch a tutor as soon as it spreads. Floette gets one or two Carbos, Gourgeist the rest.

Priorities:

- **Mat Block on Gourgeist.**
- **Nature's Madness on Floette.**
- **Nincada's moves:** Conversion, Heal Block, Trick Room and a fourth move. Trick Room is rare, so Nincada carries it for a future Sturdy Pokémon. You could skip Endure on Floette later and let Nincada learn Curse, which makes building the support a little easier.
- A **Tera Ghost shard** for Nincada.
- At least **Gastro Acid** and **Roar / Whirlwind** on Teddiursa.

Before running, ideally: **Serene Grace** and all its moves on Vivillon; Carbos and Soul Dews on Gourgeist; Gourgeist and Teddiursa fused with lures.

### Mid game: building a Sturdy Pokémon

A lot happens here, so be careful. Once DNA Splicers come more easily, watch for a biome full of **Sturdy** Pokémon (like the Construction Site loop). There, evolve **Nincada** and **fuse Floette and Shedinja**. With the free slot, **fuse Ninjask with a Sturdy Pokémon that learns Curse**: either Avalugg, Carracosta, Forretress, Regirock (hidden ability) or Steelix. If Nincada already learned Curse, any Sturdy Pokémon works. Now you have a boss killer and a support.

**Optional: keep Tera for Floedinja.** The support needs Tera Ghost to Curse, so Floedinja has no Tera left. That's fine 99% of the time. To fix it, catch another Sturdy Pokémon (ideally Steel as its first type), then either find another Tera Ghost shard for it and fuse it with Ninjask (in that order), relearn the useful moves and unfuse; or wait for a biome full of Ghost types (Temple, Graveyard), unfuse Ninjask, fuse the new Sturdy Pokémon with Ninjask and unfuse. Then **fuse with a Ghost type** that has Ghost as its first or only type, **picking the new Ghost first**. It's fiddly, so feel free to skip it.

**Pangoro** is the only wild Pokémon with Mold Breaker and a move that messes with abilities: **Entrainment**. It's no Gastro Acid, but it removes Hatterene's Magic Bounce so you can Roar it out, and helps against Gholdengo before you have Core Enforcer. **Don't use it on Uxie**: Entrainment doesn't affect passives.

### Killing bosses

Switch to **Vivillon** (if it has no Reviver Seed) or another sac, then bring the **support** in after it faints: survive with Sturdy, Tera Ghost if you aren't Ghost already, **Trick Room**, then **Curse**. If the boss has no Steel or Ghost moves and you're already Ghost, Curse immediately. Then **Floedinja**: Conversion if needed, otherwise **Heal Block**, then spam **Nature's Madness**. With Tera available, you can skip Conversion if the boss has a Steel or Ghost move but **no Flying, Fire or Rock move**: go **Tera Bug into Heal Block**. When Curse will kill next turn, sac into **Gourgeist** to restart triple lure. The same item tip as the Dark Void version applies.

### Running

Spam Run with Gourgeist and Teddiursa; use **Mat Block + Roar** to catch what you want. On X0 waves:

- Bring **Vivillon** in safely with Mat Block.
- Use **Entrainment** on **Gourgeist**, then Rock Slide / Muddy Water to protect both your Pokémon.
- Keep stalling with Gourgeist, bring the **support** in and Curse the Pokémon you don't want (if Ghost), or Tera Ghost and Curse **the next turn**. A PokeRogue bug makes Tera Ghost + Curse on the same turn target yourself.
- Switch to **Floedinja**, land Heal Block and finish with Nature's Madness.

### Should you run it?

Same strengths as the Dark Void version: no healing needed (only Lum Berries on the support, none on Floedinja), faster kills than the Leech Seed cycle, multi-hit moves handled by a Reviver Seed on the support, and X0 waves are very safe. The downsides: **five** Pokémon need passives, Eternal Floette's often needs grinding, and you rely somewhat on finding Core Enforcer (Pangoro makes that easier). Run enders are the same: Tachyon Cutter, Neutralizing Gas and Mold Breaker fusions; keep a Reviver Seed on the support, Floedinja or Teddiursa.

## Post berry rework: Serene Grace

**The berry rework isn't out yet. Don't run this until it is**: until then it's strictly worse than the builds above.

This is the best strategy after the rework (Triple lure with Serene Grace and Entrainment / Skill Swap). There are two versions with the same final build: one takes a few fewer splices, the other is more flexible about which Pokémon you use, and there's a beginner version to learn on. The Pokémon shown are recommendations; any with the right passive works.

After the rework Lum Berries remove a status the same turn it lands, so Dark Void and Powder Snow stop working, and we stall with other moves:

- **Rock Slide** instead of Dark Void. With 2 Multi Lens it flinches both targets 43.1% of the time; with **Serene Grace** from the [Prankster support](#prankster-support), 87.6%.
- **Muddy Water** instead of Powder Snow: a 30% chance to drop accuracy by one stage. With Serene Grace there's an 87.6% chance of at least one drop, 64.8% of two or more, and 21.6% of all three on both targets. With King's Rock it also flinches both 43.1% of the time.

Between these and one Mat Block, you can stall X0 waves long enough to DoT a Pokémon down, with a bit of trial and error at worst.

**Prankster options** include Clefairy (Gravity by level-up, helps carries with inaccurate moves like Kyogre and Groudon early on), Vivillon (Friend Guard hidden ability to protect your carry, Decorate and After You egg moves), and Smeargle (can be duplicated to get Sketch on another Pokémon).

Muddy Water's accuracy drops make **No Guard bad as a passive** here: No Guard means they always hit you anyway. That rules out most [waiters](#waiter) except Garchomp and Gourgeist; the others without No Guard are too slow unless you use a lot more eggs.
