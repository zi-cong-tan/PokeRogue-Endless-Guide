## Before you start

You should **not** jump into Endless right after beating Classic. To beat Endless comfortably, and to get the most out of it, you need unlocks. If you don't have them yet, read this guide anyway, plan your team, and farm the unlocks in Classic.

Many people play Endless in a way that is slow at getting shinies, unsafe, or simply won't reach the end, often by spamming legendaries or shinies. This guide teaches planned strategies built with a purpose instead. It is long, but it has everything you need to beat Endless and catch a lot of shinies along the way.

**The berry rework.** An update (1.13) will change berries so that Lum Berries are eaten mid-turn. That makes Dark Void and Powder Snow freezes useless. Teams for that update are already in [Builds](#post-berry-rework-serene-grace). You do **not** need them until the rework happens.

## The end of Endless

![The game's ending message on wave 5850: Congratulations on reaching the current end](shots/the-end-5850.png)

Endless does have an end: **wave 5850**. On that wave the game tries to give you one more [token](#tokens), but every token is already maxed out from wave 5800. This used to crash the game; now it shows a dedicated ending message.

To get there you need a good [team](#forming-an-endless-team), and a good Endless team is very different from a good Classic team. It takes anywhere from a little to a lot of preparation.

- Every **50 waves** you face a Paradox Pokémon.
- Every **250 waves** you face Eternatus.
- Every **1000 waves** you face **Eternamax Eternatus**.

After you beat these bosses, wild Pokémon gain tokens. You cannot catch Pokémon in the ??? biome in Endless or run from them under any circumstances, so your team must be able to win these battles.

## Words we use

The guide and the community use some lingo. Here it is.

- **Lure ability / passive.** A Pokémon with Commander, No Guard, Illuminate or Arena Trap. These act like the Lure item while the Pokémon is on the field. A lure passive is the same, as a passive.
- **Phaze.** A move that forces the target out of battle, like Roar, Dragon Tail or Whirlwind.
- **DoT.** Damage over time, from moves like Leech Seed, Curse and Salt Cure.
- **Carry.** A Pokémon that handles the battles while you set up your team.
- **Runner.** A Pokémon with the Run Away passive.
- **Boss killer.** A Pokémon dedicated to killing bosses late in the run, once tokens stack up.
- **Support.** A Pokémon that helps the boss killer finish a Pokémon.
- **Waiter.** A Pokémon that protects your other Pokémon from damage.
- **Tutor.** A Pokémon brought for a rare move it can teach another Pokémon.
- **Tokens.** Buffs wild Pokémon gain after every Paradox battle.
- **Carry phase.** Waves 1 to about 2000, where your carry kills things comfortably while you set up your build.
- **Run phase.** Once your build is set up and you no longer need constant access to the shop.
- **Shiny / luck fodder.** Pokémon with no value other than luck.
- **Fuse tutoring.** Fusing two Pokémon so the first one can learn a move the second has, then unfusing.
- **Sac.** Switching into a Pokémon so it faints to a hit, to get a free switch into another.
- **STAB.** Same Type Attack Bonus: a 50% damage boost when a move shares a type with its user.
- **Pivot move.** A move that faints or switches out its user, like Memento, Self-Destruct or Flip Turn. Used to bring your lure Pokémon in safely after a Paradox or Eternatus dies.

## Tokens

![The enemy token bar in game: nine token icons with their stack counts](shots/token-bar.png)

Tokens are one of the biggest differences from Classic. Every 50 waves a boss gives you at least one token when you beat it. Tokens buff **every** wild Pokémon after that. In game they show left to right in this order:

- **Recovery.** Heals the wild Pokémon 2% of its max HP each turn, per token.
- **Full Heal.** 2.5% chance each to cure any status (like burn) at the end of the turn.
- **Fusion.** Each adds a 1% chance that a wild Pokémon is fused with another.
- **Endure.** Each adds a 2% chance to endure a hit.
- **Protection.** Each cuts the damage you deal by 2.5%, multiplied together: your damage is multiplied by 0.975^X, where X is the number of tokens.
- **Damage.** Each adds 5% to their damage, multiplied together: they deal 1.05^X times more.
- **Poison.** Each adds a 5% chance to poison your Pokémon on any hit.
- **Paralysis.** Each adds a 2.5% chance to paralyse your Pokémon on any hit.
- **Burn.** Each adds a 5% chance to burn your Pokémon on any hit.

All of them cap at 10, except Damage and Protection, which cap at 999. Status tokens don't work like contact moves such as Poison Jab or Body Slam: they can apply a status on **any** damaging move.

## Getting around tokens

**Protection** makes it impossible to beat Endless by brute force through normal means: you end up doing 1 damage at most unless you use a very specific strategy. To get around it, use set damage like [DoT](#damage-over-time).

**Paralysis** is annoying because you lose turns to RNG and your speed is halved. To stay safe, fuse with an Electric type, Terastallize Electric, or use an ability like Purifying Salt that blocks all status conditions.

**Damage** tokens mean every wild Pokémon eventually one-shots your whole team. The main answer is **Sturdy**: it makes sure you **always** live on 1 HP (unless hit by a multi-hit move). Grex's [Normalise Entrainment](https://wiki.pokerogue.net/guides:endless_normalize_build) strategy is an alternative.

**Recovery** heals back up to 20% of max HP of any damage you do each turn. The [boss killer](#boss-killer) section shows every way around it.

**Full Heal, Fusion, Endure, Poison and Burn** don't matter much. Use Multi Lens against Endure, Lum Berries against poison and burn. Full Heal is somewhat helpful when stalling, and Fusion can make annoying combinations, but the builds handle them.

## Other differences from Classic

- No trainers.
- No Mystery Encounters.
- Biomes change every 5 waves.
- A Paradox Pokémon every 50 waves (forced single battle, can't be caught).
- Eternatus every 250 waves (forced single battle, can't be caught).
- Eternamax Eternatus every 1000 waves (forced single battle, can't be caught).
- After every 50 waves the enemies get at least one token.
- Lock Capsule and Tera Orb stop being guaranteed after a certain wave; they become normal rewards.
- No Premium Voucher in the rewards.
- You have **15 points** for your starters instead of 10.
- There is a final wave: 5850.
- The Map item doesn't exist.
- You *can* beat Endless by brute force, but realistically you won't. Monkee is the only person who has, with a lot of planning, teamwork and time, and it was nowhere near efficient. Play a standard team.

## Grinding to the level cap

**Why not Candy Jars?** In short, not worth the time. See [Candy Jars](#candy-jars).

**Why does Exp matter?** Early on, your carry needs to hit hard and outspeed wild Pokémon. With Lucky Eggs and Golden Eggs you can hover around the level cap. Once your waiter is fully set up, put all your eggs on it: it wants **400K speed by wave 5000**. At that speed almost nothing outspeeds you without a boosted speed stat (Regieleki might), and the extra speed helps even against speed boosts.

Levels only ever matter on your waiter and any other Pokémon you want to speed-invest.

Each Golden Egg gives 100% more Exp and each Lucky Egg 40%. You want **4400% more Exp in total**, from any mix of the two. Both stack to 99.

You get eggs from wild Pokémon. Thief, Covet and the Magician ability steal them: stall turns with Mat Block and Dark Void on your waiter while another Pokémon steals. You don't need any of those, though. You can also **catch and dump**: catch a Pokémon holding eggs, transfer the eggs, release it.

## Tips to know

- Abilities and passives that are the **same don't stack**. No Guard ability plus No Guard passive on one Pokémon counts as one lure.
- Some Pokémon are locked to a Tera type wherever they are in a fusion. Shedinja is always Tera Bug (that's why it uses Conversion), Ogerpon is always the type of its mask, and Terapagos is always Tera Stellar. If two of these are fused, the first Pokémon's lock wins: Ogerpon/Shedinja is locked to Ogerpon's mask.
- **Passives matter a lot.** A passive is a second ability. With good fusions you can have up to 11 useful abilities instead of 6. You can't get passives mid-run, so start with good ones.
- Endless is better than Classic for everything except candy grinding. For candy, read the pins in the **#game-questions** channel on Discord.
- Gigantamax, Megas, Primals and Ultra Necrozma can't Terastallize.
- **Defense does not matter.** In high waves you will **always** be one-shot because of tokens. Invest only in offense and support.
- **Is Prankster good?** For self-targeted moves like Mat Block and Conversion, yes. For status moves that target foes (Gastro Acid, Dark Void), no: Dark types are immune to Prankster, and Psychic Terrain and anti-priority abilities stop it, so it's inconsistent. Invest in speed instead.
- **Coverage is bad.** Your carry always spams one spread move with no immunities, even into a 4x resist. Coverage only matters early, while the carry is still weak.
- King's Rock doesn't work with moves that already have a flinch chance.
- Multi Lens doesn't work with multi-hit moves or multi-turn moves.
- Only **starters** can have passives. Wild bosses may show a passive but lose it when caught.
- Information from Reddit and YouTube is often unreliable unless it comes from the Discord.
- Korean has smaller text, so it loads fastest: the ideal language for speedruns.
- **Endure is the best setup move.** It leaves you at 1 HP after tanking a hit that would have killed you, then you eat berries for boosts so your carry hits even harder. Unlike Sturdy, it **does** work against multi-hit moves.
- You can give a Pokémon as many of each vitamin as its IV in that stat. 15 speed IVs means at most 15 Carbos.
- Against Sandstorm on a Sturdy Pokémon (usually your boss killer), have a weather-setting ability (Drought, Drizzle…), a fast weather move user, or Prankster. Or make the Pokémon immune: Rock, Ground or Steel.
- Low on berries while running? Catch a random Pokémon to take its berries and keep running.
- Turn **field sounds** off in settings and Pokémon skip their cries when sent out or fainting, which saves time. Muting master volume does **not** do this. Turning off move animations saves time too.
- A grey, dimmed Poké Ball next to a wild Pokémon means it has a gender, form, shiny or ability you don't have yet.
- **Sketch works like a splicer.** It replaces itself with the target's last move, and you can target your own Pokémon, so you can teach moves without using splicers.
- Items held by a Nincada when it evolves are copied onto **both** Shedinja and Ninjask, so you can duplicate items. This works with Pokémon fused to it, too.
- Fun fact: Triple Axel was named after Lululalu's dog, Axel.
